Business Problem & Solution

The Problem

Sales records span multiple years, product lines, markets, and deal sizes. Without a shared view, it is difficult to see which products and countries contribute most of the sales, how performance changes by year, and whether the dashboard’s order-level KPIs count actual orders or individual sales lines.

The Solution

I built a Tableau dashboard using the workbook’s Sales sheet, with views for yearly sales trend, sales by country, product-line ranking, and deal-size mix. KPI definitions are checked against the source fields so sales lines, distinct orders, customers, and average values are clearly separated.

  • Rank product lines and countries by total sales.
  • Compare yearly totals and deal-size contribution.
  • Distinguish line-item counts from distinct orders.

Metrics & What They Mean

Total Sales

10,032,629

Sum of the SALES field across all 2,823 sales-line records.

Sales Lines

2,823 rows

Counts source rows. The screenshot labels this as “Total Orders,” but ORDERNUMBER repeats across multiple product lines.

Distinct Orders

307 orders

Count of unique ORDERNUMBER values. This is the order count when each order number is counted once.

Distinct Customers

92 customers

Count of unique CUSTOMERNAME values.

Average Sales per Line

3,554

Average of SALES over the 2,823 rows. This is what the screenshot labels “AOV”; it is an average line value.

Order-Level AOV

About 32,680

Total Sales divided by 307 distinct orders. This derived measure is closer to average order value.

Sales by Deal Size

Sum of SALES by DEALSIZE. Medium is largest, followed by Small (2.64M) and Large (1.30M).

Yearly Sales Trend

2004: 4.72M

Sum of SALES by year of ORDERDATE. The source ends on 31 May 2005, so 2005 is a partial year.

Metric clarification: The dashboard’s 2,823 “Total Orders” value is a row count, not a distinct order count. Its 3,554 “AOV” value is average sales per row. The Sales sheet contains 307 distinct order numbers, giving an order-level average of about 32,680 (Total Sales ÷ distinct orders). Values are shown in the source workbook’s units because no currency is specified.

Key Findings

Product Mix

Classic Cars lead with 3.92M in sales. Trains are the lowest product line at 226K.

Market Mix

The USA contributes 3.63M, followed by Spain at 1.22M and France at 1.11M.

Deal Size

Medium deals contribute 6.09M, about 61% of the 10.03M total.

Year-over-Year Context

2004 is the highest full year at 4.72M. The 2005 value covers January through May and should not be compared as a full year.
